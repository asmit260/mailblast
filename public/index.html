Safety, Privacy & Credentials Guide

Understand how BulkMailer operates locally and securely.

100% Local Data Sovereignty

Unlike commercial online email platforms (which store your customer lists, campaign copy, and server details on third-party cloud servers), BulkMailer runs fully locally on your physical machine.

No External Analytics

Your API keys, lists, and SMTP passwords are never transmitted to any central database or telemetry tracker. Everything resides strictly in your RAM and disk.

Direct Outbound TLS

Connections travel directly from your server.js instance straight to your SMTP server (e.g. Google or Microsoft) via secure, encrypted TLS protocols.

Terms of Use & Data Safety Agreement: By using BulkMailer, you confirm you are sending transactional mailings or authorized promotional newsletters to opt-in subscribers only. BulkMailer enforces full compliance with CAN-SPAM and GDPR regulations by mandating local opting, custom unsubscribe headers, and direct sender verification.

Under the Hood: Transmission Lifecycle

Here is exactly what happens when you click "Send Campaign":

  1. Payload Packaging: The frontend bundles recipient addresses, personalizations, attachments, and server configs into a secure multipart/form-data stream sent to your local backend at port 3000.
  2. SMTP Handshake Pooling: The Node.js engine initiates an optimized pool (5 parallel workers max). It logs into your host and opens active secure channels.
  3. Dynamic Template Interpolation: For every individual contact, the engine reconstructs the Subject, Plain-text fallback, and HTML content, matching custom attributes (like {{name}} and {{company}}) in real-time.
  4. Spam Prevention Guard: The server injects List-Unsubscribe, Precedence: bulk, and dynamic X-Mailer headers, protecting your sender status.
  5. Batch-Throttle Controls: Transmissions are strictly grouped (e.g., 10 emails at a time) with brief rest intervals (e.g. 1000ms delay) to guarantee your outbound IP doesn't trigger SMTP host speed restrictions.

How to Retrieve Outbound SMTP Credentials

Google Gmail (App Passwords)

Gmail strictly forbids logging in with your main password for bulk apps. You must generate an App Password:

  1. Go to your Google Account Settings.
  2. In the search bar, type "App passwords" (Ensure 2-Step Verification is active on your account first).
  3. Click "App passwords", select a name (e.g. "BulkMailer"), and click Create.
  4. Copy the 16-character code shown on screen.
  5. Enter smtp.gmail.com as Host, 587 as Port, your gmail address as Username, and paste the 16-character app password as the Password!
Microsoft Outlook / Hotmail

For Outlook / Microsoft 365, set the following configs:

  • Host: smtp.office365.com
  • Port: 587
  • Encryption: STARTTLS (Secure toggle checked)
  • Login: Your complete outlook email and password. (If 2FA is on, generate an App Password in Microsoft Security Settings).
Custom SMTP (Amazon SES, Mailgun, Brevo, Hostinger)

Simply log into your custom provider dashboard, go to "SMTP Settings", and grab the credentials:

  • Host: The custom endpoint (e.g. smtp.sendgrid.net, smtp.brevo.com).
  • Port: Use 587 (STARTTLS) or 465 (SSL/TLS).
  • Username / Password: Use the custom API credentials generated on your provider's dashboard.
